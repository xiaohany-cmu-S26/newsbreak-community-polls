Mountain View council to vote on removing 62 parking spaces for El Camino bike lanes
The Mountain View City Council will decide Sept. 29 whether to proceed with the second phase of protected bike lanes on El Camino Real, a 1.1-mile stretch between Castro Street and Grant Road that would remove 62 on-street parking spaces.

City staff say the first phase, completed in 2024, cut bike-involved collisions on the corridor by 31 percent, and they recommend moving ahead. The project is estimated at $4.2 million, of which $2.9 million would come from a regional transportation grant that expires if construction does not begin by spring.

A group of business owners along the corridor has submitted a petition with 214 signatures asking the council to commission a parking study before any spaces are removed. "Our customers drive here. Take away the parking and you take away the customers," said Rosa Delgado, who owns a bakery near the Castro Street intersection.

Cycling advocates counter that the corridor is one of the most dangerous in the city for people on bikes. "Phase one worked. The data is right there," said Mark Chen of the Mountain View Bicycle Coalition. Several residents at last week's transportation commission meeting suggested a compromise that would keep parking on one side of the street and narrow the bike lane buffer.

Council members have not indicated how they will vote. Vice Mayor Priya Raman said she wants to see whether the grant deadline can be extended. The meeting begins at 6:30 p.m. at City Hall.
